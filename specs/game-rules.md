# Game Rules — Whist

Whist is a trick-taking card game with a 52-card deck for 3 to 8 players.

## What is a trick?

A **trick** is one round of card play. Each player plays one card in turn. The player who played the strongest card wins the trick and collects all the cards played. That player then starts the next trick.

The goal of the game is to predict how many tricks you will win before the round starts.

## Card Values

Cards rank by face value. The **Ace is the lowest** (worth 1) and the **King is the highest**.

If a **trump suit** is in play (see below), any trump card beats any non-trump card, regardless of value.

## Rounds

The game is played over many rounds. Each round has a **Dealer**. The dealer role passes to the next player after each round.

## The Deal

The dealer deals cards one by one to each player, mixing face-up and face-down cards:

- **Face-down cards** go into the player's hand — only they can see them.
- **Face-up cards** stay on the table in front of the player — everyone can see them.
- Every player must receive the **same total number of cards**.
- The dealer chooses how many are face-up vs. face-down. We recommend at least **5 cards per player** in total, up to the maximum a 52-card deck allows.

> **Good starting point:** 4 face-up + 5 face-down cards per player.

### Trump suit (optional, but recommended)

After dealing, set the remaining cards aside. The dealer may flip the top card — its suit becomes the **trump suit** for this round. Trump cards beat everything else.

## Contracts

Before any cards are played, each player declares their **contract**: how many tricks they think they will win. Players declare one at a time, starting with the player to the left of the dealer, going around the table.

**The dealer goes last** and has one constraint: they must choose a number that makes the total of all contracts **different from** the number of tricks in the round. This ensures at least one player will always lose points.

> Example: 4 players, 9 tricks in the round. If the first 3 players bid 2 + 3 + 2 = 7, the dealer cannot bid 2 (that would total 9). They must bid anything else.

## Playing a Round

The player to the left of the dealer leads the first trick by playing any card they choose.

Going around the table, each player **must play a card of the same suit** as the first card played — this is the most important rule.

If you have **no card of that suit**, you may:
- Play **any other non-trump card** — this card cannot win the trick.
- Play a **trump card** — this beats all non-trump cards.

The player who played the highest card of the led suit wins the trick (unless a trump card was played, in which case the highest trump wins). The winner leads the next trick.

You are allowed to start a trick by playing a trump card.

## Scoring

After all tricks are played, each player compares their contract to how many tricks they actually won.

| Result | Score |
|---|---|
| You won exactly what you bid | **+1, plus the number of tricks you won** |
| You won more or fewer than you bid | **−1, minus how far off you were** |

### Examples

- Bid 3, won 3 → **+4** ✓
- Bid 2, won 3 → **−2** (off by 1)
- Bid 3, won 5 → **−3** (off by 2)
- Bid 0, won 0 → **+1** ✓
- Bid 0, won 1 → **−2** (off by 1)

Each player's total score carries over from round to round.

## Winning

There is no fixed end condition. The game stops whenever the group decides — when the bar is closing, when a player has to leave, or simply when everyone agrees to stop.

When the game ends, the player with the highest total score wins.
